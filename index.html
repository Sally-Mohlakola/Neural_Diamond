Can a small neural network replace the light transport inside a diamond? Shown here: the exact spectral path tracer, the reference every network was measured against.

We ask whether a neural network can learn light transport inside a diamond. The neural network learns, and its deterministic decoders pick the correct exit facet more often than the stochastic decoder. But whether their diamonds matched the reference better depends on how we measured it.

Three attempts

↱ Step 1 · radiance mapAug

The average

The cloth model’s map keeps one answer per incoming direction. In a diamond, rays entering different facets aggregate it, so the shading goes flat. Sampling more rays did not help.

↱ Step 2 · LEAN-inspired spreadSep

The spread

Each bin in the radiance distribution map stores an average, so we gave back its coefficient of variation. The sparkle returns, but in random places.

The position

A network that knows where light enters, and predicts where it leaves. Shown here at its best is the deep decoder given the true exit facet for its predictions.

The neural transport operator

overview

The first surface is traced exactly. Inside the diamond, a network takes over. It decides whether the light escapes, through which facet, and where on that facet it leaves. The exit ray then goes back to the path tracer.

How the papers connect

No published paper was found to render a diamond with a neural method. But each one of the below papers gave us a piece. A model to start from, a tool to build with, or a way to measure the result.

All three decoders learn from the same data, but each places the exit differently. The stochastic decoder spreads its bets, while the clone and the deep decoder commit to a single answer.

[ the yardstick ]

How a render is scored

Pearson pixel correlation

Is the light in the correct places? 1 is a perfect match, 0 is no relation.

LPIPS

Does it look correct to a viewer? Lower is better, and 0 looks the same.

Noise floor

Two exact renders still differ a little. They correlate at 0.94–0.98.

Contrast

How much the brightness varies. Flat shading scores low. The reference GIA brilliant scores 3.72.

Exit-facet accuracy

How often light leaves through the same facet as in the exact tracer. Training rewards this.

Energy

Total light against the reference. Every decoder adds 8–46% too much.

So why do the metrics above disagree? Pixel correlation penalises a misplaced highlight twice, once where it lands and once where it should have been, so blurring pays. LPIPS rewards sharp facets, even displaced ones.

Do our results transfer to a new diamond? Shading a different diamond cut without retraining, the deep decoder keeps the old diamond's routes and surface normals.Therefore, it adds light almost everywhere onto the diamond but misplaces the bright reflections.

[ hypotheses ]

The verdict

↳ Accuracy is not appearanceUnder Pearson’s pixel correlation, the deterministic decoders that pick the exit facet most often receive the lowest scores. But under LPIPS, this order reverses.H1 · under pixel correlation
↳ Where errors ariseLonger ray paths receive higher exit-ray prediction errors. The pavilion fails only when training never reaches it.H2 · in part
↳ CostOur neural method is three times faster per ray, but 29% slower per image against path tracing.H3 · for this implementation

Limits. One lighting rig, simplified meshes, a CPU renderer, and image scores from one front view of each diamond.
